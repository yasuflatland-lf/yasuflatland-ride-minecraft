# 0181. Lay a road behind the bulldozer from the driver's held block

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-10-29
- Extends: 0033 (gear probe), 0055 (`TickContext`, `DriveStep`, `TickOutcome`, world call order), 0136 (row rounding, shared with `bladeColumns` through `feetRow`), 0137 (block replacement and drop sweeping), 0139 (the held tool becomes part of one held item value), 0178 (the shared placement-kept list), [0180](0180-a-wide-bulldozer-blade.md) (blade width)

## Context

The owner wants to clear and pave in one pass, using the driver's held block without consuming it, and to build flat bridges across liquid and roads behind the bulldozer in flight. The [approved design](../designs/2026-10-29-wide-blade-road-paver-bunk-bed.md) leaves the held-item pipeline, world call, and reference row to the builder. This decision keeps cell selection in the domain and shares the placement protection rule with World Edit.

The existing code provides these building blocks:

- `RiderView.heldTool` and `VehicleTickInput.testHeldTool` in `src/domain/vehicle/vehicle.ts`, together with `VehicleSnapshot.testHeldTool`, carry the monitor's held tool (0139). `BULLDOZER_MONITOR` lists the tools and their states; after 0178 its group is `"yasu_build:building_tools"`.
- `TickContext` and `DriveStep` in `src/domain/vehicle/drive.ts`, `TickOutcome`, and `DriveVehicles` carry drive decisions to the world calls. `DriveStep.cut` is applied after clear and before movement (0055, 0137).
- `bladeColumns` in `src/domain/vehicle/kinds/bulldozer/blade.ts` rounds the feet row and selects touching cells (0136). `bladeRule()` from 0180 supplies the normal or wide half width without a second wide-state check.
- `KEPT_BLOCKS` in `src/domain/shared/keptBlocks.ts` includes air and the four liquids as well as item holders. World Edit's `EDITABLE` in `src/domain/build/edit/guard.ts` subtracts those five open blocks again, while `BUILD_KEPT_BLOCKS` adds protection for other data-bearing blocks (0178).
- `BladeCuts` in infrastructure protects `yasu_ride:laid` and sweeps items popped from attached blocks (0137). The gear-down probe runs only during landing (0033).

## Decision

1. **Item (10/16).** Add `yasu_ride:road_paver_switch`, en_US "Road Paver", to `yasu_build:building_tools` after Wide Blade. Write the full item ID in `tools/gen_item_catalog.py`. Its shapeless recipe uses a lever, an iron ingot, and a cobblestone and is always unlocked.
2. **State and monitor (11/16).** Add property `yasu_ride:road_paver`, bool, default false, `client_sync` false because nothing on the client draws it and the monitor has its own property, and list it in `restored`. Append monitor tool `{ item: "road_paver_switch", states: ["off", "on"] }`; its screens are OFF 5, ON 6.
3. **Held item (7/16).** What a rider holds is one value object, `RiderHeld { tool?, block? }` in `vehicle.ts`, read once from the selected item's type ID by `riderHeldOf` in infrastructure. `tool` is the bare `yasu_ride` item ID the monitor uses (0139); `block` is the item's own ID when `BlockPermutation.resolve(id)` succeeds (the item ID names a block). The road lays that default permutation only, so stairs, slabs, logs, and other directional or stateful blocks lose any orientation or state; this is accepted, and the road is a flat surface. `RiderView.held` replaces `heldTool`, and `VehicleTickInput.testHeld` and `VehicleSnapshot.testHeld` replace `testHeldTool`. The core picks the driver's value, or the test value when there is no driver, once per tick; it passes `held.tool` to the monitor and `held.block` to the drive as `TickContext.heldBlock`. A passenger's held item is never used. The existing held-tool test hook becomes `TEST_HELD_ITEM_EVENT`, carries the full namespaced item ID, and goes through `riderHeldOf` like a real player. The forced changes are mechanical: the users of `testHeldTool` (`src/infrastructure/vehicle/bedrock/handlers/testHeldTool.ts`, `bedrockVehicleWorld.ts`, `driveVehicles.ts`, `ports/drive.ts`, and the test-pack scenario in `src/test-pack/scenarios/sportsboat.ts`) are renamed, and the test-event sends carry the full ID (`yasu_ride:icebreaker_switch`) and the direct `Vehicle.tick` call passes `testHeld: { tool: "icebreaker_switch" }`, with the same expectations.
4. **Accepted blocks (12/16).** `isRoadBlock(id)` in `kinds/bulldozer/paver.ts` rejects `KEPT_BLOCKS`; `yasu_ride:` and `yasu_build:` IDs; IDs ending in `_door`, `_bed`, `_sign`, `_hanging_sign`, `_banner`, or `_concrete_powder`; and an explicit list of gravity blocks, multi-cell blocks (including `minecraft:bed`, whose item and block share that ID), crops and plants that need support, and blocks that are not placeable. Support-dependent blocks such as rails, torches, and ladders are not rejected: a held block is laid with its default permutation, and the cell it goes into is checked only for protection.
5. **World call and protection (8/16, 9/16).** `DriveStep.pave?: Pave` (`{ block, cells }`, plain `Vec3` cells with no mode) passes through `TickOutcome` and is laid after `cut` and before the move, so the body moves onto a bridge laid this tick. The drive decides which cells qualify (rules 6 and 7); the adapter lays every emitted cell and only enforces protection: it skips a cell whose chunk is unloaded, that already holds the road block, that is tagged `yasu_ride:laid`, whose block is in `PLACE_KEPT_BLOCKS`, or that is camp space.

   `PLACE_KEPT_BLOCKS` is `KEPT_BLOCKS` without air and the four liquids; 8/16 splits the list once into the open blocks and `PLACE_KEPT_BLOCKS` in `src/domain/shared/keptBlocks.ts`, and World Edit's build guard uses the same list. The road keeps item holders only, not World Edit's `BUILD_KEPT_BLOCKS` (signs, spawners, vaults, command, structure, and jigsaw blocks). Open question for the owner: should the road also keep `BUILD_KEPT_BLOCKS`? On a yes, a later step moves that list to `domain/shared` and the road skips it too, recorded as an addendum to this ADR. Replaced blocks vanish without drops, and items popped from attached blocks are swept like a cut's (0137). Step 9/16 adds the world call without any drive emitting paving yet.
6. **Reference row (3/16, 12/16).** Use `feetRow(feetY, true) - 1`, the row the tracks stand on (a slab or dirt path is that row). `feetRow` in `kinds/bulldozer/blade.ts` is the rounding `bladeColumns` uses (`ceil(feetY - 0.01)` on the ground, `floor(feetY + 0.01)` in the air), extracted in 3/16, so road and cut always agree. In 12/16 the drive classifies each cell as solid, liquid, or open with its own probes (solid first, because a waterlogged block also reads as water). Per column: 2 or more higher, skip; 1 higher, lay into that block (`ref + 1`); level, lay at the reference row; liquid at the reference, lay there; 1 lower (open reference over solid), lay at the reference row; 2 or more lower, lay at the reference row only when liquid is found within `liquidSearchDepth` (8) rows below before any solid block; otherwise skip.
7. **Where (12/16).** Keep all paver tuning in `BULLDOZER_SPEC.paver`.

   - `bodyHalf` (1.7) is the half diagonal of the 2.4 x 2.4 engine collision box, rounded up, so a square of half side `bodyHalf` turned to any yaw contains the body.
   - On land, in car form with `onGround`: the strip the body has just left (the trailing side, by the sign of speed), `trail` deep behind `bodyHalf`. Over liquid only, also a support zone from `-bodyHalf` to `bodyHalf + lookAhead` ahead.
   - In hover form: the row `feetRow(feetY, false) - 1`, in a trailing strip kept `flightGap` (1.5) clear of `bodyHalf`. Each flight cell is probed with the same classifier and kept only when open or liquid, so solid blocks are left alone (about 2 x 11 probes at the wide width). The blade cut never reaches the flight strip, so a probe taken before the cut is still valid at pave time. 1.5 is at least the cell diagonal, which covers the touching-cell rule of `rectCells`, so the bulldozer never lands on its own road.
   - Takeoff, landing, submarine, and car form off the ground lay nothing. Ticks with the car off the ground (a hop up or a drop down a step) therefore lay nothing: a step leaves up to about 2 unpaved rows, and after a step up the first trailing row of the lower road may be filled up to the new level. While the bulldozer floats (water form) nothing is laid; paving resumes on ground or on its road.
8. **Gates (13/16).** The switch is on, there is a driver or test input, the held block is accepted, and absolute speed `|speed|` is above `BULLDOZER_SPEC.paver.minSpeed`, so the road is laid in reverse too (on the trailing side given by the sign of speed, rule 7). The width is the current blade rule's half width (normal or wide); raised or lowered does not matter. Blocks are not consumed in any game mode.
9. **Order (7/16, 8/16, 9/16).** These refactors change no behavior and come first. Step 7/16 permits the mechanical held-item rename and the test-input changes it forces; existing scenarios and expectations stay. The core, application, and infrastructure use kind-neutral contracts and never branch on a kind. Road cell selection belongs to the bulldozer domain, with no mode in `Pave`.

## Consequences

- Worst case about 100 cells per tick (trail strip plus support zone at the wide width), each up to 11 rows of up to 3 world reads; about 300 block reads per tick on flat land. Step 13/16 measured the slowest server tick interval during the in-game flight over a 17-block ravine at the wide width (`bulldozer_pave_flight`, 58 ms against the 50 ms tick). No slowdown was seen there, but ground paving over a deep ravine was not measured; manual row B66 records it on PC and mobile. If it is too slow, probe the support zone only when the trail strip found liquid.
- The gear probe (0033) runs only in landing, so laid road should not flicker the wheels. Flicker is only visible on a client, so manual row B69 checks it and an addendum here records the fix if it appears.
- Steps leave short gaps (rule 7).
- The shared placement-kept list prevents the road and World Edit from drifting on air, liquid, and item-holder protection. Broader protection of World Edit's data-bearing blocks remains an open question (rule 5).
