# Bulldozer wide blade, road paver, and camper bunk bed (design)

Where this design and ADRs 0180 to 0182 differ, the ADRs win: the switches join `yasu_build:building_tools` (ADR 0178), the wide blade's monitor label is NORM, the wide blade cuts 11 columns, the wide blade lengthens only the blade plate while its bolts, end plates, ribs, spill guard, and centre bracket keep their original size (the owner's decision, replacing "simply stretched"), and the kept list is `KEPT_BLOCKS`, with `PLACE_KEPT_BLOCKS` for placements such as the road.

Status: draft for review. Date: 2026-10-29.

Three independent features from one brainstorming session. Each ships as its own pull request series and its own ADR. Suggested order: wide blade, then road paver (its width follows the blade), then the bunk bed (independent, any slot).

## Intent

- **Wide blade.** The owner wants to clear land faster: a blade twice as wide as today's, swapped in and out from the cab like the blade lift.
- **Road paver.** Clearing land and then laying a road by hand is tedious. The owner wants the bulldozer to lay a road behind it while it drives, so clearing and paving happen in one pass.
- **Bunk bed.** Two players camping together should each have their own bed in the parked camper, and each bed should be their own respawn point.

## Shared constraints

- AGENTS.md and the ADRs it names apply: stable APIs only (0001), layers and features (0012, 0046), kind folders (0055), every tuning value in `BULLDOZER_SPEC` or `CAMPER_SPEC` (0002), property-based tests plus in-game `scriptevent` tests (0010), bone contract (0009), and a Resource Pack manifest bump whenever the Resource Pack changes (0015).
- Refactor before adding a variant (ADR 0050 and the owner's standing rule): whatever is fixed today and becomes switchable (blade width, ram width, the single bed) is first made a parameter in a no-behavior-change pull request, then the new variant is added.
- Everything committed is English.

## 1. Wide blade

### Behavior (decided by the owner)

- A new switch item, "Wide Blade", joins the creative group `yasu_ride:bulldozer_switches` beside `blade_switch`, and the bulldozer's cab monitor lists it as a second tool (`BULLDOZER_MONITOR`, ADR 0139/0171 `MonitorTool` switching).
- Each use by the driver toggles between **normal** and **wide**, like the blade switch. The state lives on the vehicle (an entity property), survives switching to another held item, and is shown on the monitor while the switch is held.
- Wide cuts twice today's width: today `blade.halfWidth` is 2.32 (about 5 columns); wide is 4.64 (about 9 to 10 columns). Depth (`near`, `far`) and every other blade rule are unchanged.
- The model changes with the state: wide looks like today's blade simply stretched to twice its width (no wings, no angled ends). Switching back restores both the look and the cut width.
- Wide is independent of raised/lowered: it can be toggled either way, and the blade only cuts while lowered, as today (ADR 0138).

### My calls

- The ram box (`body.halfWidth`, ADR 0006/0136) widens to the wide blade's half width while wide is on. `VehicleProfile.body` is static today, so this needs a per-tick body width or a ram width supplied by the drive; the builder picks the mechanism, without branching on kind in shared code (ADR 0050).
- Placement footprint and clearance stay as today (the blade is model only for placement, ADR 0142).
- Spawn default is normal. The entity property is a bool `yasu_ride:wide_blade`, default false, restored on rebuild like the blade lift (0127 resend after a failed send).
- Recipe: shapeless, like `blade_switch` (lever + iron ingot) plus one more iron ingot so the two do not collide; always unlocked. Names en_US "Wide Blade", ja_JP in `ja_JP.lang`. Monitor labels NORMAL / WIDE.
- The model shows one of two blade bones (`blade` and a new `blade_wide`, both children of `blade_rig` so the lift still moves them) through the render controller's `part_visibility`, driven by the property. Both bones are generated by `tools/gen_bulldozer.py`.

### Left to the builder

- Exact geometry of the wide blade (stretch the existing boxes on x, keep supports and push arms where they are or move them out; it must still read as one blade).
- Whether the README previews show the wide blade (default: no).

## 2. Road paver

### Behavior (decided by the owner)

- A new switch item, "Road Paver", joins `yasu_ride:bulldozer_switches` and the cab monitor, toggling **off** and **on**. The state lives on the vehicle like the other switches.
- While on, the bulldozer lays the block that the **driver is holding** in their selected hotbar slot. Switching the held block changes the road from that point on. Blocks are **not consumed**; the road is unlimited.
- Road width equals the current blade width: normal or wide.
- It lays whether the blade is raised or lowered.
- **On land** the road follows the terrain. For each column across the road width, the top block is replaced with the held block. The reference height is the ground the bulldozer itself stands on:
  - a column one block lower than the reference (a small hole the bulldozer rolled over) is filled, so the road is flat;
  - when the bulldozer itself steps down, the reference drops with it, so the road follows slopes down;
  - columns two or more blocks lower than the reference are left as they are (my call), unless liquid lies below them (next item).
  - a column one block higher than the reference has its top block replaced (a one-step bump stays); a column two or more blocks higher (a tree, a wall) is skipped (my call; with the blade lowered the blade has usually cut it already).
  - On land in car form, blocks are never added in open air beyond the one-block fill and the liquid bridge.
- **Over water or lava** (owner's choice "Z": a flat bridge at bank height) the road keeps the reference height. For a column whose top, at or below the reference row, is liquid, or is air above liquid, the road block goes into the reference row (the row the one-block fill uses): a lake level with the bank has its surface replaced; a river two or more blocks below the bank gets a flat bridge at bank height. Nothing is placed below that row, however deep the liquid. Over liquid the road is laid under the footprint and a little ahead as well as behind, so the bulldozer drives onto the new bridge instead of falling, floating, or sinking. Small puddles are filled the same way.
- **In flight** (flight form, ADR 0021; owner's choice "B") the bulldozer keeps flying and the road is laid in the row just below the body's bottom, but only in the cells the body has just left (behind it), never under the footprint, so the bulldozer never lands on its own road. Over a cliff, a ravine, or a drop it builds a road in the air behind itself; climbing and sinking (ADR 0032) make the road step up and down with it. Flying backward onto the road lands on it, as on any ground.

### My calls

- On land the road is laid behind the body (the cells the tracks have just left). Over liquid it is laid under the footprint plus enough ahead to stay supported at full speed; the exact look-ahead is the builder's (a tuning value in `BULLDOZER_SPEC`).
- How far below the reference row to look for liquid before a column counts as a plain cliff: a tuning value in `BULLDOZER_SPEC`, about 8 blocks. A drop deeper than that, or with solid ground first, is a cliff and gets nothing.
- While the bulldozer is floating (water form) the paver lays nothing; it resumes once the bulldozer is back on ground or on its road.
- Replaced blocks vanish without drops, as in a blade cut (ADR 0137).
- Never replace kept blocks: the `CUT_KEPT_BLOCKS` list (containers and other item holders) and anything tagged `yasu_ride:laid`. Liquids are not kept for the paver; replacing a surface liquid block is the point.
- Lay nothing when the held item is not a single-cell placeable block: empty hand, a switch, a tool, a door, a bed, a tall plant, and similar. The builder defines the accepted set (for example, item IDs that resolve to a block type whose placement is one cell) and tests it.
- Lay only when the bulldozer has a driver and is either in car form (on the ground, or on its own bridge) or in flight form. Not while floating or under water, not while taking off or landing.
- In flight the road replaces air and liquids in its row, never solid blocks; a solid block in the way is left as it is and the road continues past it. The gear-down probe (ADR 0033, 4 blocks) will see the road behind the body; check in game that the wheels do not flicker, and if they do, record the fix in the ADR.
- Lay only while moving (reuse the blade's `minSpeed` idea with its own value), so standing still does not keep rewriting the same cells.
- Default off on spawn and on rebuild unless the property says otherwise. Property `yasu_ride:road_paver`, bool.
- Recipe: shapeless, lever + iron ingot + one cobblestone; always unlocked. Names en_US "Road Paver", ja_JP in `ja_JP.lang`. Monitor labels OFF / ON.
- Works in every game mode, Survival included, like the blade cut.

### Left to the builder

- How the held block is read and passed to the drive (the monitor already reads the driver's held item), and how a held item ID maps to a block permutation (default state).
- The world call used to place blocks (per cell `setType`/`setPermutation` or a fill), and how it fits the per-tick world call order of ADR 0137.
- How the "reference height" is taken from the bulldozer's feet, and the behavior on partial blocks (slabs, dirt path), consistent with `bladeColumns`.

## 3. Camper bunk bed

### Behavior (decided by the owner)

- The parked camper's single bed (ADR 0092; `furniture.ts` bed slot, `tools/gen_camper.py` `BED`) becomes a two-level bunk bed in the same place: the existing lower bed plus an upper bunk above it.
- The bunk is built from thin panels (a thin upper deck with a mattress, slim posts or side panels), so it reads as a bunk bed without filling the cabin.
- Each level is its own bed: long-pressing or using it sets that player's spawn point to that level and runs the existing sleep flow (fade, skip the night, "You can sleep only at night" otherwise). Two players can each have their own bed and spawn point.

### My calls

- Level choice is by aim: the upper bunk has its own block cells, so pressing the upper bunk sleeps there and pressing the lower one sleeps below. The owner suggested "same button, upper if lower is taken" as a fallback if aiming is hard; aim is used because "taken" has no stable meaning (sleep is a 2 s fade, nobody stays in a bed). Revisit if aiming at the upper bunk proves hard on a phone.
- The upper bunk occupies the two cells above the bed (local x 1, z -2 and -3, layer 2) with a new or reused hidden bed block whose collision and selection box match the upper mattress. Those cells move from the interior/cargo set into the room, as the lower bed's cells did (ADR 0092), and the cell counts in that ADR change accordingly.
- Neither level leaves room to respawn standing on the mattress: the upper bunk's block cannot reach below its own cell, so it sits at least 1.0 block above the floor, a player on the lower mattress (0.56) would reach about 2.36, and the ceiling is 2.81 above the floor. So each level sets the spawn point to its own aisle cell (local x 0) beside the bed, on the floor: the lower bed to z -2, the upper bunk to z -3. The two spawn points are distinct, so "each bed is its own respawn point" holds. This changes ADR 0092's lower-bed spawn point; record it in the bunk ADR.
- No ladder entity or climbing; the upper bunk is used by pressing it from the floor.

### Left to the builder

- Exact heights of the upper deck, mattress, and posts, within the headroom and without hiding the existing wall cabinet or ceiling lights (ADR 0103, 0115); move or drop anything above the bed that collides and record it in the ADR.
- Whether the driving (unparked) model also shows the bunk (default: yes, same as today's bed).

## ADRs

One ADR per feature, each written in the first pull request of its series (README in `docs/adr` explains how): wide blade (extends 0136, 0138, 0139), road paver (extends 0137, 0139), bunk bed (extends 0092).

## Tests

- Domain: property-based tests for wide/normal blade columns, road column selection (land reference height, one-block fill, two-block drop left alone, liquid surface with look-ahead), and bunk cell layout.
- In game: toggling each switch, a wide cut, paving over land, a one-block hole, a step down, a water crossing that does not sink, and sleeping on each bunk level with distinct spawn points.
