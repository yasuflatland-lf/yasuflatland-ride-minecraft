# Beach Parasol and the Resort Gear group: design

Date: 2026-11-09. Status: draft for the owner's approval.

## Intent

The owner wants a new Resort Gear group in the creative catalog, beside Camping Gear (Outside), and a Beach Parasol as its first item. The parasol goes over a table and chairs, and the owner plans to stand many of them along a beach. More resort items, such as a beach lounger, will join the group later, so the group and the code must take new items without rework.

The parasol is a cantilever (offset) parasol: the pole stands to one side and an arm holds the canopy over the seating area, so nothing stands in the middle of the table. The owner supplied three reference photos. They are product photos, so they stay out of the repository; the builder reads them from the owner's machine at `~/Downloads/`:

- `parasol_side_whole.png`: the whole parasol from the side.
- `parasol_blueprint.png`: plan and side drawings. Canopy diameter 3500 mm, total reach 3745 mm, total height 3160 mm, canopy edge height 2340 mm, base plate width 1070 mm.
- `parasol_base.png`: the base from above and to the left, close up.

The model must match these photos closely. That is the main quality bar.

## Goals

1. A new creative group, Resort Gear (`resort_gear`), placed right after Camping Gear (Outside) in `GROUPS` of `tools/gen_item_catalog.py`. It holds `yasu_ride:beach_parasol_item`, which is also its icon. Lang: en_US "Resort Gear" / "Beach Parasol"; ja_JP uses the Japanese words for "resort goods" and "beach parasol" (the owner's own names for them).
2. A Beach Parasol entity whose model reproduces the photos (see Model).
3. A regular octagonal canopy built from 8 panels, large enough that a 3 by 4 block area fits fully under it.
4. Dye paint recolors the canopy fabric only.
5. Rain does not fall under the canopy.
6. Three hits on the base take the parasol back, with a message.
7. Survival players get one parasol on their first join, and can also craft it.

## Non-goals

- The canopy does not rotate, tilt, open, or close. The crank in the photo is decoration.
- No collision on the pole, arm, or canopy. Players walk through every part. The base has no physics collision either; it only needs a hit box for taking the parasol back.
- No shade or light change under the canopy beyond stopping rain.
- No beach lounger or other resort item in this work. Only the group structure that lets them be added later.

## Anti-goals

- A parasol that looks like a generic stick-and-cone umbrella. It must read as the photographed cantilever parasol: the leaning wooden pole, the dark steel arm, the hanging octagonal canopy, and the stone-slab base.
- Rain-blocking blocks left behind in the world after the parasol is taken back, or invisible blocks that players bump into.
- A parasol that cannot be placed over a table and chairs because its own pole or base lands on them.

## Size and proportions

- One block is 1 m (16 px). The canopy is a regular octagon whose flat-to-flat width is 5 blocks (80 px). This is the smallest regular octagon that contains a centered 3 by 4 block rectangle (the corner at (1.5, 2) needs a half-width of at least 3.5/√2 ≈ 2.47). The owner gave "a 3 by 4 area fits under it"; 5 blocks is my call from that.
- All other dimensions scale uniformly from the blueprint, so the proportions match the photos. 5 m canopy against the real 3.5 m is a scale of about 1.43, which gives roughly: canopy edge 3.3 blocks above ground, top of the arm 4.5 blocks, base-to-canopy-center offset about 3 blocks, base plate about 1.5 blocks wide. Whatever the rounding, every base and pole cell must lie more than 2 blocks from the canopy center along the facing axis, so the base clears a 3 by 4 layout in either orientation (my call). My call: uniform scale over the lower 2.6 / 3.5 block heights shown in the first mockup, because the owner asked for an accurate reproduction. The builder may round these to whole pixels.
- The canopy's octagon has two flat sides facing the four grid directions, as in the plan drawing, so a 3 by 4 table layout sits square under it.

## Model

Generated by a new `tools/gen_beach_parasol.py` (model front at -z, per ADR 0009), with its geometry, texture atlas, and item preview produced by `pnpm model` like the other camp gear. Parts, from the photos:

- **Base** (`parasol_base.png`): a dark charcoal steel tray, L-shaped in plan, with a low raised lip. On it lie four granite slabs, two side by side and two layers high, speckled light grey with visible edges between them. They sit behind the post socket, away from the canopy. A square steel socket rises at the tray's front, with two black round knob screws: one on the socket, one on the lower post.
- **Lower post**: dark charcoal steel, rising from the socket and bending once, so the upper part leans back, away from the canopy.
- **Pole**: a warm orange-brown wooden pole continuing the lean, with a black crank handle partway up, and a thin wooden brace running from the pole's middle up to the arm.
- **Arm**: dark charcoal steel, from the top of the pole out over the canopy center, rising slightly, with a short vertical drop to the canopy hub. A thin light stripe runs along its top edge, as in the photo.
- **Canopy**: 8 fabric panels meeting at a center hub, sloping down to the edge. A small raised vent cap sits on top. Underneath, 8 wooden ribs run from the hub to the edge, with a dark hub cone below and small end caps at each rib tip. Bedrock cubes cannot be triangles, so each panel is assembled from tilted cube strips (see `tools/cube_tilt.py` and `tools/panels.py`). The exact panel construction is the builder's choice, as long as the octagon outline and the 8 seams read clearly from below and from the side.
- **Colors**: canopy fabric is a warm off-white (the photo's "white"), the underside slightly darker. Wood, steel, and granite colors are sampled from the photos.

**Fidelity check.** Before wiring behavior, the builder renders previews (`tools/render_preview.py`) from the same three viewpoints as the photos and shows them to the owner side by side with the photos. The owner approves the look, then the rest proceeds. Expect a round or two of adjustments.

## Placement

- Using the item on the top face of a block places the parasol with the **canopy center** above the pressed cell. The base lands about 3 blocks farther away from the player, along the player's facing direction snapped to a quarter turn. The owner chose this ("B") so they can tap the middle of their table area.
- Using the item on a Camp Table entity does nothing special; the owner does not want table-tap placement.
- The base needs a solid top face at the same height as the pressed cell, and clear air for the base and pole cells, outside vehicle space and tent camp space. Otherwise placement is refused with an action-bar message, and nothing is consumed. Chairs and tables under the canopy are fine, because the canopy and arm claim no cells.
- My call: a new parasol is refused if its base cell is within 1 block of another parasol's base. Canopies of neighboring parasols may touch or overlap, so the owner can line them up along a beach.
- Survival consumes the item; Creative does not. The same per-player, per-tick dedupe and deferred placement as other camp gear apply.

## Rain

Bedrock rain falls per column and entities cannot block it (ADR 0207). While a parasol stands, it lays an invisible rain-blocking block in every column that overlaps its octagon, in the first cell above the canopy's top. Rules follow the roof tent rain ceiling:

- Only air cells outside vehicle space are filled. Others are skipped.
- Laid cells are recorded on the entity and cleared when the parasol is taken back or removed. Only cells that still hold the ceiling block are cleared.
- A restore scan re-lays missing cells after reload or restart and clears stray ones.

Unlike the roof tent, the ceiling stays as long as the parasol stands, not only while someone is inside.

**Block choice.** The owner wants no collision. My call: a new `yasu_ride:parasol_ceiling` block with no collision box, no selection box, no light emission, and invisible geometry. **Risk:** it is not confirmed that Bedrock rain stops at a block with no collision. The first implementation step is a spike on the dev BDS. If rain passes through such a block, fall back to a thin plate block with the same 3 px collision as `tent_ceiling` but fully invisible (no visible texture; the owner asked for "a transparent, invisible plate"). It sits just above the canopy, high above the players. The owner's words were "the canopy should keep the rain off; nothing else needs collision", so collision at canopy height is acceptable and the spike result is a branch, not a blocker. The builder records which block was used in the ADR.

## Paint

- The parasol follows `PAINTS` with sixteen `yasu_ride:paint_<paint>` groups and events and a render controller, as the tent does (ADR 0135).
- The default paint is `white`, showing the warm off-white of the photo. A white dye restores it.
- Only the canopy fabric texture slots (top and underside) are recolored. Wood, steel, granite, ribs, and caps keep their colors in every paint. Generator validation rejects a fabric slot outside the canopy bones, as `gen_tent.py` does.
- The shared dye rules apply: dye mapping, cost, no-op when the color already shows, and one dye per tick. A dye hit never counts toward taking the parasol back.

## Taking it back

- Three non-dye hits on the parasol within the shared hit window fold it. The hits land on the base (the entity's only hit box). Each hit before the last shows a hint message, as the Dutch Oven and Fire Pit do. The last hit shows a taken-back message.
- Folding clears the rain ceiling, removes the entity, and returns one plain `beach_parasol_item` to the hitter. Paint is lost, as with the tent (my call, for consistency).

## Getting one

- **Starter item:** on a player's first Survival join (`initialSpawn`), give one Beach Parasol, following the roof tent starter (ADR 0200) with its own player flag, for example `yasu_ride:beach_parasol_starter_received`. Existing players without the flag get one on their next join. Creative players are not given one or marked. A failed give retries next join, and a full inventory drops it at the feet.
- **Recipe:** a shaped crafting-table recipe from white wool (canopy), sticks (pole), iron ingots (arm), and stone slabs (base weights). The exact pattern is the builder's choice. It must not collide with a vanilla or existing add-on recipe.

## Code placement

The owner chose a new `resort` feature in every layer (`src/domain/resort`, `src/application/resort`, `src/infrastructure/resort`), wired through its own `installResort` called from `bootstrap()` (ADR 0046). Beach loungers and other resort items join this feature later.

Features cannot import each other, and the parasol needs machinery that camp owns today. So the work starts with a refactor that changes no behavior, in its own commit or pull request, before any parasol code (the same order ADR 0050 asks for vehicle kinds):

- Move the machinery the parasol reuses from `camp` into `shared/` in the same layer. Candidates, to confirm while planning: ground placement (`application/camp/groundPlacement.ts`), taking back by hits (`application/camp/takeBackByHits.ts`), the rain ceiling geometry and its lay, record, clear, and restore-scan adapter (`domain/camp/roofTent/ceiling.ts` and the roof tent occupant world), and the first-join starter give (`giveStarterRoofTent.ts` and its port and adapter). Generalize each only as far as two users need. Camp keeps its behavior and its tests stay green.
- `bootstrap()` passes the vehicle space and the camp space checks to `installResort`, as it does for camp, so a parasol base is refused in vehicle and tent space.
- `pnpm lint:deps` rules gain the `resort` feature like the others.

Tuning values go in a `PARASOL_SPEC` in `src/domain/resort/`.

## Left to the builder

- How each canopy panel is built from cubes, and pixel-level dimensions after rounding.
- The recipe pattern.
- Message wording (English and Japanese lang entries).
- Sound effects, reusing existing camp gear sounds if none fit better.

## Summary of my calls (for the owner to check)

1. Canopy flat-to-flat width of 5 blocks, derived from the 3 by 4 requirement.
2. Uniform scaling from the blueprint, which puts the canopy edge at about 3.3 blocks and the top at about 4.5. This is higher than the first mockup, for fidelity.
3. Bases must be at least 2 blocks apart. Canopies may overlap.
4. A collisionless invisible rain block, with an invisible thin plate as a fallback if the spike fails.
5. Paint is lost when the parasol is taken back.
